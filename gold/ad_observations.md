## Hook patterns (first 3 seconds)
1. **Open mid-confrontation, never on a title card.** The first frame is already the conflict: someone is being humiliated, slapped, accused or thrown out in front of others.
2. **A shock line or reveal as the very first audio.** A short, loaded sentence that raises a question ("He's the CEO?!", "You were never my daughter.").
3. **Status reversal set up immediately.** The underdog is shown being looked down on, and a hint of hidden power (a ring, a title, a powerful ally arriving) promises the payoff.

## How they end
- On an **unresolved beat**: a door bursts open, an order is shouted ("Arrest her!"), a shocked face, a question nobody has answered yet.
- Then a CTA card ("What happens next? Watch the full series on the app"). The clip itself never shows the answer.

## Length and structure
- Mostly short vertical video (9:16), typically ~30–60 s for paid social.
- Three common shapes: (a) one continuous scene (humiliation → turn); (b) a **jump-cut** inside one episode with the slow middle removed; (c) a **cross-episode "arc"**: early humiliation, then a later payoff or reveal, ending on a cliff.
- Ad creatives are ~89% video for ReelShort. Meta/Facebook is the largest network (~43% of creatives), with Moloco, InMobi, TikTok and YouTube Shorts also used.
- Many cuts per show are A/B tested; variety of hooks matters as much as any single "best" clip.

## Implications for the proposer prompt
- Prefer candidates whose **first block** is already conflict (an accusation, a strike, a public rejection, a reveal line), not setup.
- Prefer endings where the **next line would answer the question**, and cut before that line.
- Visual hooks (a strike, an entrance, an object reveal) count even without dialogue.
- Produce **varied** candidates (different beat types), not near-duplicates.
